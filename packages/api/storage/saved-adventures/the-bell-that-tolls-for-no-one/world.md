# The Bell That Tolls for No One

For three nights running, the bell of Saint Merewen's church has rung itself at midnight, though the rope was cut years ago and the churchyard gate has been chained since the last priest was buried — or rather, since he walked out of his own grave. The villagers of Hollowmere, who light candles in their windows and refuse to speak after dusk, have pooled what little coin they have to hire outsiders willing to enter the grounds and silence the bell before the fourth night, when the oldest graves — the ones with no names, only dates — are said to open. The party expects to find a haunt, a curse, or some necromancer squatting in the ruin; what they carry instead is holy water, silver, and the uneasy knowledge that every corpse in that graveyard was buried with its coffin nailed shut from the outside. Fog clings to the leaning headstones, crows watch from the steeple, and somewhere below the frost-heaved earth, something patient is counting the hours until moonrise.
