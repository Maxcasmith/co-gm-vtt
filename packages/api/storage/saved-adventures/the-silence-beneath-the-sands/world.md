# The Silence Beneath the Sands

For three thousand years the tomb of a pharaoh whose name was chiseled from every obelisk and temple wall has lain sealed beneath the dunes, its location dismissed by scholars as a grave robber's tall tale — until a sandstorm stripped the desert down to bedrock and revealed a black granite doorway where no doorway should be. The party has been hired by a nervous patron of antiquities to reach the burial chamber before looters, tomb-robbers, and rival expeditions descend on the site. They carry torches, pry bars, and a translation of the warning inscription above the lintel, which promises that the one buried here does not sleep, and does not forgive. The desert air is furnace-hot and utterly still, but the draft seeping from the cracked seal is cold, and it smells faintly of myrrh and old decay. Whatever riches sleep inside, the party suspects the pharaoh's name was erased for a reason.
